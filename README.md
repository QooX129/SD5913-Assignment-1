## Designers and Artists
Designers and artists share a common foundation: both start with an idea and use creative decisions to turn it into a material or digital form. At the center of both practices is communication between the creator and other people. Designers and artists use their work to convey messages, experiences, emotions, and ways of understanding the world. This people-based approach is also reflected in the Design Council's (n.d.) principles of putting people first and communicating visually and inclusively. Both require imagination, technical skill, observation, and an understanding of how others may experience the work. 

However, their methods and intentions can differ. A designer generally communicates a message or solves a practical problem for a particular audience, client, or user, while an artist more often communicates personal ideas, emotions, or interpretations of the world. Although the two roles can overlap, design is usually guided by function and specific requirements, whereas art allows greater emphasis on personal expression and open interpretation. For example, Tate (n.d.) describes generative art as computer-based art made through predetermined systems, showing how artistic communication can also involve rules, technology, and audience participation.
## Why Programming 
Programming connects closely with both design and art because it enables ideas to be developed into interactive and functional digital experiences. Beattie (2020) presents code as an expressive medium, suggesting that this practice involves creative choices about structure, interaction, and the experience produced for others. It gives creators another way to communicate with people by allowing an audience to interact with, influence, and respond to the work. For designers, programming provides greater control over structure, behavior, accessibility, and the way users interact with a product. For artists, it can serve as a creative medium through which they explore movement, sound, images, systems, and participation. 

Programming is important because it allows creators translate concepts into precise instructions. As Lovelace (1843) argues, a machine can execute what people know how to order it to perform, but it does not originate ideas independently. Programming also allows creators to test and revise their work in response to users or changing conditions, an approach consistent with the Agile Alliance's (n.d.) emphasis on working software, changing requirements, and regular reflection. Therefore, programming is not only a technical skill; it is also a means of extending creative communication and strengthening the relationship between an idea, its creator, and its audience.
## Creativity and AI
AI can support creative work, but it does not necessarily produce genuinely new ideas. A study discussed by Georgetown University examined more than 370,000 college admissions essays written before and after the launch of ChatGPT in 2022. The essays written after the launch used a greater diversity of words, yet they often contained fewer original ideas (Georgetown University, 2026). This finding is important because people commonly associate varied language with creativity; however, the study found that readers rated ChatGPT-assisted essays as more creative even when the ideas themselves were not more original. This does not mean that AI always limits creativity. Its effect depends on how the user works with it: relying on AI to create the main ideas may reduce originality, while using it to test, develop, or refine personal ideas can support creativity.
## Why are we here
We are here to learn programming because artists and designers need to understand and control the tools they use. AI may generate code or suggest ideas, but it cannot decide what we want to communicate or why it matters. Learning programming helps us judge AI’s results and use them in a way that supports our own creativity. AI does not have to limit the user; the result depends on whether the user gives up the creative process or uses AI as a tool. This is why programming remains important for artists and designers.
