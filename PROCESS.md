## Dylan Beattie, *The Art of Code* (video)

- [Core idea] Programming can be an expressive medium as well as a practical
	profession. Beattie compares code with other creative forms: its rules and
	constraints do not prevent expression, but give artists material to work with.
	Programmers make choices about structure, elegance, timing, interaction, and the
	experience produced for other people.
- [Connection to the essay] A program is not only a set of instructions that
	achieves an outcome. Learning to code can help a designer shape the behaviour,
	limits, and character of a digital work, including work made with AI tools.

## Ada Lovelace, Note G to *Sketch of the Analytical Engine*

- [Core idea] Lovelace distinguishes between a machine carrying out operations
	and a machine originating ideas. The Analytical Engine "has no pretensions
	whatever to originate anything": it can do what people know how to order it to
	do, but it cannot anticipate new analytical truths. However, translating ideas
	into instructions can reveal new relationships in a subject.
- [Connection to the essay] Note G warns against both exaggerating and dismissing
	automation. A machine's output still depends on human judgement about the problem,
	the instructions, and the meaning of the result.

## Principles behind the Agile Manifesto

- [Core idea] The twelve principles describe software development as an ongoing,
	collaborative practice, not a one-time act of producing code. They prioritise
	frequent delivery of working software, collaboration, responding to change,
	sustainable pace, technical excellence, simplicity, and regular reflection.
- [Connection to the essay] Programming ability includes communication and revision.
	A useful program must continue to serve real people as their needs change, which
	challenges the idea that generated code alone completes the work.

## Don Ihde, mediated perception

- [Core idea] Ihde's postphenomenology argues that technologies are not neutral
	objects placed between people and the world. Technology mediates perception and
	action: it magnifies some possibilities, reduces others, and shapes what users
	notice, value, and imagine they can do.
- [Connection to the essay] An image or code generator is not merely a fast
	assistant. Its interface, training data, defaults, and output style influence
	the work a designer is likely to make. Programming can help a designer notice
	and contest these mediations rather than accepting them as invisible.

## Alan Turing, *On Computable Numbers, with an Application to the
Entscheidungsproblem*

- [Core idea] Turing explains computation through a simple abstract machine that
	reads, writes, and moves across symbols according to precise rules. Although the
	model is deliberately limited, a universal machine can follow the encoded
	instructions of other such machines.
- [Connection to the essay] The paper frames a computer as a system that executes
	formal procedures, not as an all-purpose mind. It makes specification central:
	powerful systems can perform complex operations, but someone must still decide
	what process to describe and how to judge the result.

## What I Kept and Rejected

> [Kept] Lovelace's distinction connects directly to contemporary AI. A system may
> generate a result, but it does not remove the designer's responsibility to frame
> the problem and evaluate the output. This is a precise idea that can be tested
> against an example from my own practice.

> [Rejected] Programming is valuable only because AI cannot write code. That claim
> would be quickly outdated and avoids the assignment's harder question. A stronger
> argument needs to address how programming changes a designer's judgement,
> authorship, and relationship to the tools they use.
